Planning & risk

How do you build a plan when the requirements are vague at the start? planning▸
PreBank: vague board ambition → scoped, demo-first delivery plan. The skill is sequencing under ambiguity — you don't wait for perfect requirements, you scope a first provable increment (the demo), define what it must answer, and let that retire the biggest unknowns before committing the heavier build. Name it as a deliberate de-risking sequence, not improvisation.
Walk me through how you manage risk on a project. risk▸
Don't say "I keep an eye on risks." Say: a risk register where each item has an owner, a likelihood/impact read, and a mitigation or contingency — not just awareness. Reviewed on the steering cadence, not when it's already a fire. The AWS/SES bounce-rate episode on Mulala is a concrete micro-example: an operational risk that hit, and I handled the appeal with Amazon directly rather than letting it stall the product — evidence you run the operational layer, not just the Gantt chart.
A senior stakeholder wants something that breaks the plan. What do you do? escalation▸
Not "I push back" and not "I just do it." You quantify the trade-off and hand the decision up with a recommendation: "This adds X to scope and moves the date by Y; here are two options and the one I'd recommend, and why." Controlled scope variation with a documented decision — the sponsor owns the call, you own that it's made consciously and recorded. This is exactly the register that signals seniority.
